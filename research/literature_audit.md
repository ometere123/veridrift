# VERIDRIFT literature and originality audit

Audit date: 16 November 2026 (Africa/Lagos). This is a targeted, non-systematic search of 2024–2026 work, supplemented by the sprint resources and older conceptual precedents. Searches covered multi-agent LLM handoffs and relays, propagation and conformity, reported confidence, source dependence and attribution, and evidence-responsive revision. Links point to experiment authors, proceedings, or organizers. This audit summarizes published work and does not report VERIDRIFT measurements.

## What the sprint examples already propose

The following are worked project ideas on the official Resources tab. Treat them as design precedents with underlying references, rather than as completed evaluations with results. [Apart Research, AI × Epistemics Research Sprint](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-x-epistemics-research-sprint-2026-11-13-to-2026-11-15)

| Example | Overlap | What VERIDRIFT measures differently |
|---|---|---|
| MisleadBench | Multi-turn discrepancies between communicated certainty and evidence that can be defended. Builds on MASK and DarkBench. | Scores explicit claim status and confidence after sequential handoffs, against the case's source ledger. It does not test misleading implication or intentional lying. |
| Confidence Audit | Elicits confidence in recommendations and checks what it tracks. Uses examples such as ForecastBench. | Measures stated confidence in claims supplied with bounded evidence and separates packet sufficiency from factual truth. It has no real-world outcome horizon or general calibration claim. |
| FlipRate | Distinguishes warranted updating from agreement with successive rebuttals. Builds on SycEval and ELEPHANT. | Uses new primary records at a fixed stage to test warranted updating, and an evidence-free branch to measure drift. A confidence change can matter without an answer flip. |
| Provenance Study | Matches tasks with and without machine-readable provenance signals and checks citation quality. | Tests case-local source identifiers plus an explicit uncertainty-preservation protocol in sequential handoffs. The source identifiers are references, not authenticated signatures, credibility scores, or production attestations. |

## Closest published and preprint approaches

1. **Gao et al. (2026), Copies or Sources? Measuring How LLM Aggregators Count Restated Evidence in Multi-Agent Systems.** This is the closest prior result. Controlled logs, web copies, and agent-generated logs show that model-reported probabilities count copies as partial extra evidence. Agent-team topologies include a relay chain. A protocol instructing workers to refer to readings rather than restate them reduced premature commitment while retaining sensitivity to distinct observations. The controlled testbed has a Bayesian reference posterior and the aggregators see the full logs. VERIDRIFT applies related concerns to semantic claim sufficiency, distinction between an unresolved and a refuted claim, stagewise model-produced assessments, late decisive correction, and the comparison between provenance alone and provenance with explicit uncertainty. It cannot attribute any effect to copy counting by itself. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06192) · [Methods and appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06192v1)
2. **Bara (2026), Epistemic Sybil Resistance: Multiplying AI Agents Without Multiplying Evidence.** This work formalizes evidence ancestry and tests posterior coverage while varying evidence roots and report multiplicity. Its shared-root model shows a further nuance: multiple imperfect extractions can recover distinct information from one source, subject to a source-level ceiling and correlated errors. In VERIDRIFT, no-new-evidence means no new external record. A confidence increase alone therefore does not establish that an agent simply double-counted an endorsement. The primary endpoint requires an affirmative high-confidence classification on a claim still unsupported or contradicted by the complete stage-specific packet. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01873)
3. **Anghel et al. (2026), ClaimArena-LLM: Auditing Adversarial Propagation of False Causal Attributions in Multi-Agent LLM Debates.** It scores source existence and semantic support separately using predefined support and contradiction annotations, tracks how a specified false attribution spreads, and uses matched case experiments. VERIDRIFT similarly checks source-to-claim alignment. Its narrower setting focuses on ordinary sequential transfer without a planted adversarial agent, explicit certainty on open claims, and a source intervention that can change evidential status. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.3390/make8100327)
4. **Yoffe, Amayuelas and Wang (2025), DebUnc: Improving Large Language Model Agent Communication With Uncertainty Metrics.** Confidence transmission is established prior art. DebUnc estimates agent confidence and conveys it by prompting or attention weighting, evaluating debate task performance. VERIDRIFT makes verbalized confidence part of a provenance-bearing claim state and examines behavior under held or updated case evidence. Its confidence fields are neither token probabilities nor independently calibrated uncertainty estimators. [EMNLP Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1265/)
5. **Liu et al. (2026), PropUQ-MAS: Propagation-Aware Uncertainty Quantification for LLM Multi-Agent Systems.** This approach models a communication graph and combines local and inherited uncertainty for step-level reliability estimation. VERIDRIFT evaluates the observable consequences of changing handoff prompts in a fixed topology. It does not build a general reliability estimator for agent graphs. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22130)
6. **Hao et al. (2026), Not All Flips Are Conformity: Decomposing Stance Convergence in Multi-Agent LLM Debate.** Separates isolated self-reflection, peer stance, and full reasoning in matched conditions. Shifts can occur without peer exposure, so a handoff effect should not automatically be called conformity. VERIDRIFT scores evidence sufficiency separately from answer movement. A clean decomposition would also need a budget-aligned repeated inference or self-reflection control. [arXiv preprint, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00820v2)
7. **Mohamed et al. (2025), LLM as a Broken Telephone: Iterative Generation Distorts Information.** Demonstrates cumulative information distortion through translation relays, and studies prompt-based mitigation. VERIDRIFT measures how relayed assessments treat individual factual claims and respects uncertainty as a desired output under insufficient evidence. A chain can lose details without amplifying confidence, or become excessively certain while retaining the same wording. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20258)
8. **Wan et al. (2026), The Deliberative Illusion: Diagnosing Factual Attrition and Stance Homogenization in Multi-Agent LLM Deliberation.** DelibTrace tracks survival of atomic, issue-critical facts and position diversity across deliberation rounds. VERIDRIFT uses predefined candidate claims, stage-specific source sufficiency and elicited confidence. Its ordinary arm may lose source details as well as convey endorsement, so these explanations can coexist. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03032)
9. **Yan et al. (2026), When Truth Is Distributed: Misinformation Derails Collective Fact Recovery in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems.** ForesightSafety-TIDE matches honest conditions to deception by a key evidence holder, tracing testimony adoption and evidential roots as agents aggregate partial observations. VERIDRIFT concerns unsupported certainty in ordinary transfers and compares protocol variants under held or updated source evidence. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03421)
10. **Schuster, Gautam and Markert (2026), Whose Facts Win? LLM Source Preferences under Knowledge Conflicts.** Controlled RAG experiments show repeated claims from less credible source types can overturn model preferences for more credible sources. This supports distinguishing repetition from source strength. VERIDRIFT uses stage-specific source records and generated inter-agent handoffs instead of contrasting institution or author types. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03746)
11. **Wu, Wu and Zou (2024), ClashEval: Quantifying the Tug-of-War between an LLM's Internal Prior and External Evidence.** Tests whether provided documents induce useful or harmful departures from a model's prior answer under controlled perturbations. In VERIDRIFT, messages from an upstream agent are derivative and the positive and retraction controls use authoritative new case records. [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/3aa291abc426d7a29fb08418c1244177-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)
12. **Xiong et al. (2024), Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty? An Empirical Evaluation of Confidence Elicitation in LLMs.** Shows that black-box confidence elicitation depends on method and task, and documents overconfidence with direct self-reports. This motivates treating the scores in VERIDRIFT as elicited outputs. A threshold on reported confidence is an operational rubric, not a guarantee of posterior calibration. [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6733cf15e10e2cd1d59af033c3bb8507-Abstract-Conference.html)
13. **Keramati et al. (2026), The Confident Liar: Diagnosing Multi-Agent Debate with Log-Probabilities and LLM-as-Judge.** Relates intermediate reasoning-token probabilities and evaluator scores in a two-agent constructor–auditor setup. VERIDRIFT scores elicited claim-level confidence and evidence status against fixed labels rather than interpreting a generation token's likelihood as confidence in the claim. [ACL Student Research Workshop](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.121/)

## Further close precedents found by citation and title search

- **Nnamdi et al. (2026), Epistemic Erosion: An Error Class Beyond Hallucination in LLM Text Generation.** Measures escalation, attenuation, omission and other changes in the expressed certainty of aligned source claims during summarization and rewriting. Surviving claims tend to shift toward more certainty. VERIDRIFT connects elicited confidence to stage-specific evidential sufficiency and directly tests whether a chain preserves the ability to update when a decisive record arrives. Amplification through rewriting itself is already an empirical observation in this prior work. [IJCNLP–AACL proceedings](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aacl-long.223/)
- **Gong et al. (2026), When Upstream Messages Override Correct Answers: A Controlled Study of Multi-Agent LLM Collaboration.** Crosses fixed receiver input with absent, original or conclusion-inverted upstream messages, observing both useful and harmful revisions. This is a precedent for measuring a handoff against a no-message control. Its result concerns answer substitution under a single controlled transfer. VERIDRIFT instead treats unresolved claims as valid states and evaluates provenance and uncertainty instructions under staged evidence. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36855)
- **Kaiser Sun et al. (2026), Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict.** Tracks conflict detection, resolution and communication in individual agent trajectories under controlled and natural evidence conflicts, with matched no-conflict examples. It reports cases in which an agent detects uncertainty but does not convey it in its final response. VERIDRIFT tests loss of uncertainty across separate agent contexts and compares explicit handoff protocols. [EMNLP Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.2128/)
- **Basta et al. (2026), Correction or Manipulation? Measuring Epistemic Vigilance in LLM Agents Under User Pressure.** Separates sensitivity to warranted correction from the general tendency to concede. An evidence-gated revision prompt mainly changed concession propensity. VERIDRIFT uses agent handoffs in place of user pressure, but shares the methodological point that confirmation and retraction performance must accompany evidence-free certainty metrics. [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36453)


## Contribution and claim boundaries

The mechanism and general mitigation are established prior work. Agent reports can repeat evidence, confidence can grow with repetition, and provenance rules and uncertainty transmission have been studied. The sprint contribution is a compact matched diagnostic of **when a model in a relay treats a semantically unresolved or refuted claim as established**, and whether adding explicit uncertainty instructions to provenance changes that behavior while retaining response to genuinely decisive new evidence. Positive and negative branches are jointly informative. An agent that refuses all claims can avoid unsupported certainty while failing the confirmation and retraction controls.

A protocol contrast measures the combined differences in prompts and transmitted messages. Explicit fields, citation requirements, instruction length, access to source content, summary loss, copying earlier numbers and additional generation opportunities can all affect responses. A decisive new record changes the operational evidential label under the synthetic case rules. There is no Bayesian target probability for a statement supported by those rules. A finite paired case set estimates performance on these tasks and this workflow, with no assumed generalization to other topologies, models, human recipients, or evidence streams.
